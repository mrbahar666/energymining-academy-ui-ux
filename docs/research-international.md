# International Research — Energy Mining Academy UI/UX

**Date:** 2026-11-25
**Purpose:** Study live international academy / self-development / course / self-assessment / book-selling platforms and decide what to borrow for Energy Mining Academy (Sayful Alvi) — self-knowledge → self-change; Human Assessment (210 questions, 7 layers × 30) + 26 self-tests; Four Energy courses (Voice / Body / Mind / Purpose); books; Personal Project Plan; spiritual lessons. Audience: Bangladesh, all levels, Muslim + non-Muslim, adults / teens / children, mostly mobile, slow internet. Courses + books SELL and lead the site; assessment is COMPLETELY FREE (no paywall cues, no price) with its own menu section and exactly ONE homepage section. Brand colours only: Navy #032C42, Off-white #F6F3EC, Gold #C9A24B, Charcoal #1C1C1E. Bilingual bn/en, Bangla first.

## How I searched

I ran web searches for each platform type (personal-growth course platform, free personality test, celebrity-taught courses, online course marketplace, meditation/wellbeing app, personality-assessment platform, Bangladesh bookshop) and then opened the real homepages (and, where the homepage was a seasonal promotion, a real category or test page) with the text-fetch tool on 2026-11-25. Everything below is what I actually read on those pages or in search results returned for them. Where a page could not be read properly, I say so instead of describing it. No prices are quoted except where the page itself stated them at the time of reading; all of these sites were running Black Friday promotions on the day of reading, so their homepages were promotion-heavy — that itself is a finding (see "Avoid" notes).

Sites studied (8): Mindvalley, 16Personalities, MasterClass, Coursera, Headspace, Udemy, Truity, Rokomari.

## 1. Mindvalley — https://www.mindvalley.com/

**What it is:** Online personal-growth learning platform. Programmes ("Quests") taught by well-known teachers, across categories Mind, Body, Soul, Career, Relationships, Entrepreneurship, plus meditations, events and coaching certifications. Membership gives access to the whole library (110+ Quests from 100+ teachers, 2,500+ lessons, about 20 minutes a day).

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen, 2026-11-25):**
- The homepage on the day I read it was a Black Friday takeover: hero "Your Greatest Self, at Our Lowest Price of the Year", 60% off membership, $199 for the whole year (normally $499), offer ends 30 November, 15-day money-back guarantee.
- "What's Included" list: 110+ Quests from 100+ teachers; 2,500+ lessons, about 20 minutes a day; live classes and workshops with the teachers; EVE, an AI guide to the right Quest; every new Quest added automatically.
- Named featured Quests: 6 Phase Meditation, Vivid Vision, Smart Money, The Champion Mindset, Total Self-Confidence, plus "100+ more programs INCLUDED".
- Teacher wall: named teachers (Jeffrey Allen, Vishen Lakhiani, Regan Hillyer, Ken Honda, Jim Kwik, Marisa Peer, Paul McKenna) "+ 100 more".
- Founder's handwritten note (Vishen Lakhiani) as a trust element.
- FAQ section answering credibility, what Mindvalley does, refund policy, and pointing to a free quiz ("take our free Life Mastery Score quiz to discover programs tailored to your specific needs").
- Category pages (Mind, Career) list Quests as cards with teacher name, a short description of what the learner will learn, and a "Learn More" link; some Quests state a duration in days (e.g. a 6-day programme).

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **Category-led course catalogue (Mind / Body / Soul / Career / Relationships).** Maps directly onto our Four Energy courses (Voice / Body / Mind / Purpose): four clear energy categories as the main way to browse courses, instead of one long mixed list.
- **Quest cards with teacher name + one plain-language outcome line.** Our courses are multi-teacher; a card that says who teaches it and what changes for the learner fits our course detail pages exactly.
- **"About 20 minutes a day" lesson rhythm.** Short daily lessons suit our mobile, slow-internet audience and our spiritual-lesson / daily-practice content far better than long lectures.
- **Free quiz as a gentle router to programmes.** A short free quiz that points a visitor to the right course is a good model for how our free self-tests can lead (without pressure) into a course — as long as the test itself stays free and the recommendation is honest.
- **Founder's personal note as trust.** Sayful Alvi is the face of the Academy; a short personal note from him on the home page would do the same job as Vishen's note.

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Seasonal discount takeover of the whole homepage.** On the day I read it, almost the entire homepage was one sale. Our owner has rejected designs three times and wants a shopfront that sells courses and books every day; a countdown-sale homepage would bury the journey, the books and the free assessment.
- **All-access membership as the only way in.** Mindvalley sells one membership for everything. Our model is per-course / per-book purchase (teacher-set pricing, no payment UI yet), so a single-subscription frame does not fit.
- **"Lowest price of the year" urgency language and a renewing discount.** Fake/forced urgency conflicts with our no-fake-timers rule and with the trust-first brand (Navy = trust).
- **AI guide (EVE) choosing the Quest for you.** We should not add an AI recommender; a simple free self-test result is the honest router for us.

## 2. 16Personalities — https://www.16personalities.com/

**What it is:** Free personality-test platform (NERIS model, five scales, 16 types). The test is free, takes about 10 minutes, needs no account; income comes from paid extras (Premium Career Suite, team reports, coaching tools).

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen):**
- Hero: a user quote ("It's so incredible to finally be understood."), "Only 10 minutes to get a 'freakishly accurate' description of who you are and why you do things the way you do", one big "Take the test" button.
- Live counters: "251K+ tests taken today", "201M+ tests taken in United States", "1.59B+ total tests taken", "91.2% accuracy rating".
- "Personality types — Understand others": free type descriptions that explain what drives, inspires and worries each type, to build more meaningful relationships.
- "Teams — Understand your team better": Team Assessments for communication and strengths, for teams of all sizes.
- Testimonials: real users shown with photo, name, type code and nickname (e.g. Benny, Architect, INTJ-A; Nicole, Advocate, INFJ-T), with a note that customers receive a reward for sharing feedback, positive or negative.
- Closing repeat of the single call to action: "Curious how accurate we are about you? Take the test."
- Result pages (seen via user screenshots in search results) show: type name + nickname, trait sliders with percentages per dimension, "Your Strengths" and "Your Weaknesses" lists, and sections for Career Path, Personal Growth and Relationships — with repeated prompts to "Unlock full results" for a fee.

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **One hero, one action, one time promise ("Only 10 minutes").** This is the cleanest free-test entry I saw anywhere. Our free Human Assessment section should copy this shape: what it is, how long it takes, one button — but for us it must be exactly ONE homepage section, not the hero (courses + books lead).
- **Results as a map, not a verdict: traits + strengths + watch-outs + growth.** Our guide already forbids a single total score, ranking and diagnosis labels; 16Personalities' per-dimension profile with strengths and growth areas is the closest live model for our Human Assessment / self-test result pages (per-layer view, no total score).
- **Free type library ("Understand others").** A browsable library of the 16 types is a good model for a browsable library of our Human Types / Four Energy Map — free reading that teaches the model before anyone takes the test.
- **"Understand your team" as a secondary audience.** A small parallel of this — family / classroom use of the free tests — could fit our teens/children audience later, but only as a minor section.
- **Testimonials with type + name, and an honesty note about rewarded feedback.** If we ever show learner words, this disclosure habit is the honest way to do it (our plan currently allows no invented testimonials at all).

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Big live counters and an "accuracy rating" (91.2%).** Our plan forbids invented counters; a "tests taken" counter would be invented for us, and a percentage accuracy claim for a self-knowledge tool would be a false precision claim. Do not copy.
- **"Unlock full results" paywall on the result page.** This is exactly what our owner forbade: our assessment is completely free, so the result must be complete on the free page, with no locked sections and no price.
- **"Freakishly accurate" marketing claim.** Over-claiming accuracy for a self-reflection tool breaks trust; our copy must stay modest ("a map to reflect on", not a verdict).
- **Paid Career Suite / Teams upsells woven into the free result.** Keep our free result page clean: the only next steps are the journey (courses, books, Personal Project Plan), not a paid unlock.

## 3. MasterClass — https://www.masterclass.com/

**What it is:** Online learning platform where classes are taught by famous experts/celebrities (founded 2014). Each class is a set of on-demand video lessons plus supporting material such as a workbook. Library of 200+ classes across 13 categories.

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen — limited read):**
- The homepage text fetch returned almost nothing except a repeated Black Friday banner ("Get 50% off before Black Friday", "Offer ends soon"). The categories page (masterclass.com/categories/design-creative-arts) likewise showed mainly the same promotion, the line "UNLEASH YOUR CREATIVE SPIRIT with the world's best creatives", "Starting at $10/month (billed annually) for all classes. 30-day money back guaranteed", instructor images (Kelly Wearstler, Anna Wintour), and the prompt "What do you want to learn today?". The course catalogue itself did not render in the text fetch, so I am not describing its homepage layout beyond this.
- Category list (from the site's own categories page via search): Acting & Performing Arts, Art & Design, Business & Entrepreneurship, Community & Government, Film & TV, Food & Drink, Games & Digital Media, Health & Wellness, Music, Outdoor Adventures & Events, Science & Technology, Sports & Athletics, Writing.
- Lesson format (from independent reviews of the platform): each class typically about 3–5 hours of video, broken into 10–20 minute lessons, with a downloadable class workbook; the site saves where you stopped, even mid-lesson; learners can discuss with fellow learners in a Community Discussion area; occasional live classes with instructors.

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **Teacher-first course pages.** The instructor IS the product: name, face, and what they teach. Our multi-teacher platform needs exactly this — a teacher profile that carries the course, with the teacher's own dashboard behind it.
- **Short lessons (10–20 minutes) + a downloadable workbook.** Fits our mobile audience and slow internet, and a workbook is a natural companion to our courses and to the Personal Project Plan.
- **"What do you want to learn today?" as a browsing prompt.** A plain, friendly entry question for the course catalogue — better than a bare search box for first-time visitors.
- **Resume-where-you-left-off.** Our My Courses page should remember lesson progress; MasterClass shows this is a standard expectation, not a luxury.
- **Community discussion per course.** A modest discussion space per course would fit our learning community, if moderated and kept simple.

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Celebrity as the whole brand.** Our authority is Sayful Alvi and his teachers, not fame; copying the celebrity-glamour look would misrepresent a self-knowledge academy.
- **Subscription-only access ("all classes" for a monthly price).** Does not fit our per-course / per-book purchase model.
- **Homepage reduced to a sale banner.** Same warning as Mindvalley: when I read it, there was effectively no homepage content beyond the promotion. Our homepage must still sell and explain on a normal day.
- **No certificate included with membership (separate paid certificate courses).** We have no certificates in the plan at all; do not invent a certificate upsell.

## 4. Coursera — https://www.coursera.org/

**What it is:** Large online course platform partnering with universities and companies (Google, IBM and others). Courses, Specializations, Professional Certificates and degrees; Coursera Plus is an annual subscription for unlimited access to thousands of courses. Some courses are free to audit (no certificate); first module previews are free.

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen):**
- Hero: "Learn without limits", plus a Black Friday promotion for Coursera Plus ($239/year).
- "AI is reshaping work — Build the AI skills that matter for your role" and an "AI Series" featured collection.
- "Coursera for Teams" block (gift your team new skills, save 40%).
- **"What brings you to Coursera today?"** — four goal choices: Start my career / Change my career / Grow in my current role / Explore topics outside of work.
- "Get job-ready for an in-demand career — No prior experience needed to get started", with a linked claim "91% of learners achieved a positive career outcome".
- "Why people choose Coursera": learner stories with name, photo and a quote (Sarah W., Noeris B., Abdullahi M., Anas A.) about confidence, career change and mentoring others.
- Long FAQ answering accreditation/recognition of certificates, whether a certificate is worth it, what Coursera Plus is, free courses, most popular courses, job help, and Coursera for Business.
- FAQ confirms: some free courses exist (no certificate); many courses let you preview the first module free; financial aid is available.

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **"What brings you here today?" goal picker.** This is the best homepage routing idea I saw. Our 6-stage journey (Life Timeline → self-knowledge → Vision → Development → Cleansing → spiritual path) can be offered as 3–4 plain goal choices ("Know myself", "Grow one energy", "Plan my life", "Learn spiritually") so a first-time visitor lands on the right part of the journey instead of guessing.
- **Guided pathways (Specializations / Professional Certificates): a named sequence of courses toward one outcome.** Our Four Energy courses + Personal Project Plan should be presented the same way: not four loose courses, but a visible path (e.g. Voice → Body → Mind → Purpose, then My Life Plan) with progress.
- **Free first-module preview.** A free first lesson of each paid course is an honest, low-pressure way to sell courses — it fits our "free-first" spirit without giving the whole course away and without a discount.
- **Thorough FAQ as a trust section.** Our Help/FAQ page should answer the real questions (what the assessment is/isn't, what a course includes, formats, refunds) in plain language, as Coursera's does.
- **Learner stories tied to a concrete change.** If we ever add learner words, Coursera's pattern — name + what actually changed (confidence, mentoring others) — is the honest template. (None invented; only real words.)

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Outcome statistics ("91% of learners achieved a positive career outcome").** A self-knowledge academy cannot honestly promise a percentage outcome; do not copy outcome stats.
- **Certificate / accreditation as the main selling point.** Our courses are about self-change, not employability credentials; leading with certificates would mis-sell them.
- **Heavy Black Friday / subscription promotion above the content.** Same caution as the other platforms.
- **"For Business / For Teams" enterprise funnel.** Not our audience; our audience is individuals and families in Bangladesh.

## 5. Headspace — https://www.headspace.com/

**What it is:** Mental-health app for meditation and sleep (founded 2010). Subscription app (1,000+ expert-led exercises, sleepcasts, soundscapes, wind-downs, mental-health coaching), plus a separate offer for employers/health plans. Also sells student, family and gift plans.

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen):**
- Hero promotion: "50% off a year of Headspace. Feel your best for our best price. Limited time only."
- **"What kind of headspace are you looking for?"** — six need chips: Stress less, Sleep soundly, Manage anxiety, Process thoughts, Practice meditation, Start therapy.
- "Members are enjoying happier and healthier lives" (member stories section heading).
- "Trusted by thousands of employers, health plans, and partners globally — Support your team today with mindfulness, coaching, EAP, therapy, and psychiatry."
- FAQ: what Headspace is, its mission ("to revolutionize mental health for humanity and to guide you to more joy, less stress, and the best sleep of your life"), how to download the app, what a subscription includes, cost, auto-renewal, cancellation, and team support.
- App-store rating shown: 4.7/5 (1,284,000 reviews).

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **"What kind of headspace are you looking for?" need chips.** This is the single best small interaction I saw for our site: six plain need-words that route a visitor to the right content. Our equivalent is a "How are you feeling / what do you need right now?" row on the home page (e.g. "Know myself", "Calm my mind", "Find my voice", "Plan my life", "Sleep / rest", "Learn spiritually") routing to a self-test, a course, a book or a lesson. It is simple, mobile-friendly and needs no account.
- **Short, named practices (sleepcasts, wind-downs, soundscapes).** Our spiritual lessons / daily practices should be packaged the same way: short, named, repeatable audio-friendly pieces, not long articles.
- **Mission stated in one plain sentence.** Our home page should state the Academy's purpose in one sentence the same way ("self-knowledge → self-change") instead of hiding it in a paragraph.
- **Clear FAQ including cancellation/renewal.** Even though we have no payment UI yet, the habit of answering the awkward questions plainly (what it costs, how to stop) builds the trust our brand promises.

**Avoid — and why:**
- **App-only delivery and subscription auto-renewal.** Our audience is on the mobile web with slow internet; the Academy must work fully in the browser, no app install, no auto-renewing subscription.
- **"Start therapy" as a need chip.** We are not a therapy or medical service; our guide forbids diagnosis/disease labels. Never present a test or course as therapy, and never route distress toward a product — our safety rule (no sales card where a low wellbeing score appears) must stand.
- **Employer / health-plan B2B section.** Not our audience.
- **App-store rating as a trust badge (4.7/5, 1.28M reviews).** We have no such rating; do not invent one, and do not design a slot that invites inventing one.

## 6. Udemy — https://www.udemy.com/

**What it is:** Large online course marketplace. Individual courses (per-course purchase, not subscription-only), taught by many independent instructors, with certificates of completion; also sells certification exam preparation and a business offer.

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen):**
- Hero: "Learn *essential* career and **life** skills — Udemy helps you build in-demand skills fast and advance your career in a changing job market."
- "Join others transforming their lives through learning": learner stories with photo, name and role (e.g. Cris M., Google AI Essentials graduate; Alvin Lim, Technical Co-Founder/CTO; William A. Wachlin, Partner Account Manager at AWS; Ben C., Google AI Professional Certificate graduate), each describing a concrete use within a day or a new role.
- "Get certified and get ahead in your career": certification prep with practice tests and exam vouchers; certification badges shown grouped by area (CompTIA: A+, Security+, Network+, Linux+, PenTest+; AWS: Solutions Architect, SysOps, DevOps Engineer, Security Specialty, Data Analytics; PMI: PMP, CAPM, PMI-ACP, PMI-PBA, PMI-RMP, PgMP).
- "Popular Skills" section heading at the foot of the fetched text.

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **Per-course purchase with the instructor named on the card.** This is the closest marketplace model to ours: each of our Four Energy courses is bought on its own, taught by a named teacher who sets the price and enrolment (our guide's multi-teacher model). Udemy proves the pattern visitors already understand.
- **Learner stories that name a concrete, fast, real-world use.** ("Within 24 hours, I had a functional, highly useful app for my law firm.") Our course pages should invite this kind of concrete outcome story from real learners — never invented.
- **"Popular Skills" as a simple browse row.** Our equivalent is a "Popular right now" row of the four energies / most-read books — simple, honest, and easy on mobile.
- **Certification-prep grouping (badges grouped by area).** The grouping idea (not the certificates) fits our self-tests: group the 26 self-tests by area (Voice / Body / Mind / Purpose, plus Life Timeline and 360° Mirror) so visitors can find the right test at a glance.

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Career / employability as the whole frame.** Udemy's hero, stories and certification blocks are all about getting a job or a new role. Our promise is self-knowledge and self-change; copying the career frame would mis-sell the Academy.
- **Certificate of completion as a headline benefit.** We have no certificates in the plan; do not add one to look like Udemy.
- **Marketplace noise (countless courses, constant discounting).** Udemy's catalogue is huge and sale-driven; ours is deliberately small (4 courses, a growing book list) and trust-led. Keep the catalogue calm and curated.

## 7. Truity — https://www.truity.com/ (assessment platform)

**What it is:** Personality-assessment platform (since 2012). Free tests (Enneagram, Big Five, Myers-Briggs-style TypeFinder and others); basic results free, full in-depth report paid; also sells team/workplace assessments (Truity @ Work) and coaching tools. Over 50 million people have taken Truity assessments, per its own pages.

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen — limited read):**
- The homepage text fetch returned mainly press quotes about Truity (from Inc., The List, DatingAdvice, LinkedIn, HuffPost, CNBC, Chicago Tribune, Business Insider, Entrepreneur and others) rather than the site's own navigation or section list, so I am not describing the homepage layout beyond that. The test page below is the solid evidence.
- Enneagram test page (truity.com/test/enneagram-personality-test), read in full:
  - Header facts: "388,431 tests taken in the last 30 days"; "This free Enneagram personality test will show you which of the 9 personality types suit you best. See how you score for all 9 Enneagram types".
  - "Clinically reviewed" badge (reviewed by Dr. Steven Melendy, PsyD).
  - "To take the Enneagram test, mark each statement based on how well it describes your personality."
  - Long explainer sections: What is the Enneagram? (nine types, core motivations, desires and fears; used in counselling, business and spiritual settings); The Nine Enneagram Types (each type in 2–3 sentences, with its core fear); Enneagram Triads (Heart / Head / Body centres of intelligence); Wings; Arrows (growth and stress directions).
  - "Why Should I Take the Enneagram Test?" and "Why Choose Truity's Enneagram Test?" (research depth, developed by personality experts, serious about science, easy to use).
  - FAQ: what the test is based on; accuracy (claims publicly available reliability/validity data and a 4.9/5 user rating); length (about 105 questions, 10–15 minutes); "Is this a free Enneagram test? Yes, this Enneagram test is free to take and receive your basic results. If you choose, you can also purchase a comprehensive premium report."; what results look like ("free basic test results showing how you scored for each of the nine types" + option to unlock the full report for a fee); account saves progress as you go (without an account, progress is not saved); workplace use; "None of our tests are appropriate for children under the age of 14", under-18s only with parental guidance.

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **Teach the model BEFORE the test (what it is, the nine types, triads, wings, arrows).** This is the best assessment-education pattern I saw. Our Human Assessment page should do the same: explain the 7 layers and the Four Energy Map in plain language with diagrams, before asking anyone to start 210 questions. It lowers fear and raises completion.
- **"Scores for ALL types", not one label.** The free result shows how you scored for each of the nine types. Our result should likewise show all seven layers / all four energies — a profile, never a single type stamped on the person.
- **Progress saved for logged-in users; guests must finish in one sitting (stated honestly).** Our assessment flow needs the same honest rule, stated before starting: log in to save progress across sessions.
- **Named expert reviewer ("Clinically reviewed by Dr. …").** If a qualified reviewer ever reviews our instruments, naming them builds trust. (No reviewer exists yet — do not invent one.)
- **Clear age guidance in the FAQ (not for under-14s; under-18s with parental guidance).** Our audience includes teens 13–17 and children 10–12, so we need the same kind of plain age guidance, adapted to our own instruments, on the test pages.
- **Sample report link ("see what you can expect from your full report").** A sample Human Assessment report (with blurred/placeholder data) is already in our guide's homepage plan; Truity confirms visitors want to see the result before committing time.

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Free basic results + paid full report ("unlock your full report for a fee").** This is the exact paywall pattern our owner forbade. Our assessment is completely free: the full result is free, no locked sections, no price anywhere on the assessment flow.
- **"Most accurate test" / reliability-claims marketing.** Truity leans on validation data and a 4.9/5 rating; we must not make accuracy claims we have not measured, and we have no user rating to show.
- **"388,431 tests taken in the last 30 days" live counter.** Same rule as 16Personalities: no invented counters on our site.
- **Press-quote wall as the homepage.** A wall of press logos/quotes is not evidence for a new academy and would look borrowed; our trust must come from the founder, the content and real learners.

## 8. Rokomari — https://www.rokomari.com/ (book-selling platform, Bangladesh)

**What it is:** Bangladesh's largest online bookshop (per its own homepage: "more than 300,000 books in almost 3,000 categories"), also selling electronics, gadgets, stationery and more. The reference model named in our own design plan for the bookshop section. Cash on delivery across Bangladesh and to more than 30 countries, per its homepage.

**Homepage + main sections (as actually seen — partial read):**
- The homepage text fetch returned mainly the site's SEO description text and a long "Top Writer" list (Arif Azad, Humayun Ahmed, Muhammed Zafar Iqbal, Farid Ahmed, Munzereen Shahid, Dr. Khandaker Abdullah Jahangir, Shaykh Ahmadullah, Tamim Shahriar Subeen, Ahmed Sofa, Paulo Coelho, Dale Carnegie, Brian Tracy and many others), plus the helpline "16297" and hours "Saturday to Thursday, 9am to 11pm". The visual homepage sections (banners, category rows, bestseller carousels) did not render in the text fetch, so I am not describing them.
- Book detail pages (read via search results for live Rokomari product pages):
  - Title + format in the heading, e.g. "Textbook of Orthopedics (Paperback)".
  - Price in taka (e.g. TK. 3190; TK. 1431), "Buy Now" button, "In Stock (only 1 copy left)" / stock notes, and an estimated delivery line ("Inside Dhaka: tomorrow …; outside Dhaka: the day after …").
  - "Product Specification & Summary": a plain description of what the book covers, plus chapter/section structure.
  - "Related Products" row (other books by topic/author, with price).
  - Specification table: title, category, language, number of pages, publisher, ISBN, binding/cover material, paper weight, weight.
  - Promotions shown on product pages include app-first-order free shipping over ৳1199 (promo code APP1ST) and payment cashback offers with countdown timers (e.g. "10% cashback on bKash payment on orders over ৳799 … 39 days : 21 hours : 05 minutes : 11 seconds remaining").
  - Some books note a long sourcing delay ("It will take 30–40 days to collect this book from a foreign supplier").

**Worth borrowing — and why:**
- **Book detail page shape: cover + title + author + format + price + Buy Now + stock + delivery estimate + summary + specification table + related books.** This is the exact template our book detail page (books/atmar-rosayon.html) should follow — it is what Bangladeshi book buyers already know how to use. Our formats are PDF / hard copy / audio (per our guide), so the format choice replaces Rokomari's hardcover/paperback choice.
- **Specification table (pages, language, publisher, ISBN, binding).** Factual, useful, and easy to fill honestly for our books; it also gives the page real content without invented praise.
- **"Related Products" row.** Our books, courses and the Personal Project Plan should cross-recommend each other ("Readers of this book also take the Voice course") — the order-bump idea our plan already wants, done the Rokomari way.
- **Delivery estimate in plain words (inside Dhaka / outside Dhaka).** Our buyers are in Bangladesh; a plain delivery line on the book page (once real delivery is set up) removes the commonest pre-purchase question.
- **Helpline number and hours in the header/footer.** A phone number with hours is the right contact pattern for our audience (many will call rather than email). Ours must be the Academy's real number once confirmed — not Rokomari's 16297.

**Avoid — and why:**
- **Countdown timers on payment cashback offers.** Fake/forced urgency; our plan forbids timers. Never copy a ticking countdown.
- **"Only 1 copy left" stock pressure.** With one real book and print-on-demand/PDF formats, scarcity pressure would be invented. Show honest stock only ("In stock" / "Out of stock"), never a fake low-stock warning.
- **Promo-code and cashback clutter on the product page.** Our plan has no payment UI yet and no discount system; a wall of promo codes would confuse a first-time buyer and cheapen the brand.
- **300,000-book scale signals (huge category counts, "Top Writer" walls).** We have 1 real book so far; a giant catalogue frame would highlight emptiness. Keep the bookshop calm and honest: a small, growing shelf, with placeholders clearly marked as placeholder.
- **Selling electronics/gadgets/stationery alongside books.** Our bookshop sells the Academy's books only; do not broaden it into a general store.

### Deliberately NOT adopted

- All-access membership / subscription-only access (Mindvalley, MasterClass) — we sell per course / per book.
- Paywalled results or "unlock full report" (16Personalities, Truity) — our assessment is completely free.
- Live counters, accuracy percentages, outcome statistics, app-store ratings (16Personalities, Truity, Coursera, Headspace) — no invented numbers, ever.
- Countdown timers, "lowest price of the year", "only 1 copy left", promo-code clutter (Mindvalley, MasterClass, Coursera, Headspace, Rokomari) — no fake urgency, per our design rules.
- Celebrity-glamour branding, career/employability framing, certificates as the headline (MasterClass, Udemy, Coursera) — our promise is self-knowledge and self-change.
- Therapy/medical framing or "Start therapy" routing (Headspace) — we are not a therapy service; no diagnosis labels, no sales card on a low wellbeing score.
- Leaderboards, ranking, single total score (none of the sites above centre these, and our guide forbids them) — results stay a profile.
- App-only delivery / auto-renewing subscriptions (Headspace) — the Academy must work fully in the mobile browser on slow internet.
